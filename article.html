Flávio Bolsonaro takes commanding lead in Brazil election

Currency strengthens as first round puts son of jailed former president in pole position against President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva

Flávio Bolsonaro took a commanding lead in the first round of Brazil's presidential election on Sunday, putting the son of the jailed former president in pole position for a run-off against Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva later this month.

With almost all ballots counted, the senator had secured a share of the vote well above pre-election polling, a result that sent the real sharply higher in early Asian trading as investors bet on a more market-friendly administration.

Lula, who is seeking a fourth term, conceded that the result was "harder than expected" but told supporters in Brasília that the second round would be "a different contest".

Analysts said the scale of the lead would force Lula's campaign to rethink its strategy in the three weeks before the run-off. "This is no longer a referendum on the incumbent. It is a referendum on the Bolsonaro brand," said one São Paulo-based political consultant.

Flávio Bolsonaro has promised to cut spending, accelerate privatisations and restore what he called "fiscal sanity", while distancing himself from the more combative rhetoric of his father, who is serving a sentence for his role in the events following the 2022 election.
